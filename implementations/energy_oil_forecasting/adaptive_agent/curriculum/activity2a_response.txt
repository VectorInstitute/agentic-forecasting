## 2. Systematic Patterns Identified

### Pattern A: Systematic Interval Over-Conservatism (Too Wide)
* **Finding:** The 80% prediction intervals are systematically too wide across all horizons, leading to actual coverages of **91.5% (7d)**, **93.6% (14d)**, and **98.0% (29d)**, significantly exceeding the 80% target.
* **Horizon Scaling:** The over-conservatism scales progressively with the forecast horizon. At **7d**, the intervals are **50% wider** than needed (10.94 actual vs. 7.30 needed). At **14d**, they are **90% wider** (15.55 actual vs. 8.20 needed). At **29d**, they are **108% wider** (22.31 actual vs. 10.72 needed), more than double the required width.
* **Regime Impact:** This over-conservatism is most severe in the **"medium" volatility regime**, where coverage reaches **94.1% at 7d**, **97.1% at 14d**, and a perfect but highly inefficient **100.0% at 29d**. Under standard market conditions, our model's statistical intervals are excessively wide.

### Pattern B: Systematic Upward Point-Forecast Bias (Over-Forecasting)
* **Finding:** There is a consistent positive bias (forecast − actual) across all horizons, meaning the model systematically over-forecasts prices.
* **Horizon Scaling:** The bias worsens significantly at longer horizons, moving from **+0.44 at 7d** to **+0.86 at 14d** and peaking at **+1.49 at 29d**.
* **Regime Impact:** This upward bias is heavily concentrated in the **"elevated" volatility regime** at short-to-medium horizons (**+1.68 at 7d** and **+1.58 at 14d**) and the **"medium" volatility regime** at the longest horizon (**+1.96 at 29d**).

### Pattern C: Negative Relative Skill vs. Naive Baseline
* **Finding:** The AutoARIMA model exhibits negative relative skill against a simple `last_value_naive` (random walk) baseline at **7d** (MAE 2.40 vs. 2.34) and **29d** (MAE 3.71 vs. 3.59), and virtually identical skill at **14d** (MAE 2.80 vs. 2.81).
* **Implication:** The model's complex trend projections and mean-reversion calculations are actively degrading point forecast accuracy. This indicates that a simpler momentum-smoothed trend or naive baseline would yield lower errors, primarily because the model is chasing upward trends that do not materialize.

### Pattern D: Opposite Directional Bias in Extreme Volatility (N=2)
* **Finding:** In the very small sample of extreme volatility origins ($N=2$), the model exhibits a negative (under-forecasting) bias across all horizons: **-1.35 at 7d**, **-1.21 at 14d**, and **-1.19 at 29d**.
* **Implication:** While $N=2$ is too small to build a major correction on, it suggests that under extreme stress (shocks), the model fails to keep pace with the upward price spikes, contrasting sharply with its usual over-forecasting behavior in normal or moderately elevated environments.
