### I. Analysis of Systematic Patterns & Market Context Synthesis

The 2025 backtest reveals two major systematic failures in the AutoARIMA statistical baseline:

#### 1. Severe Over-Conservatism in Prediction Intervals (Overestimation of Uncertainty)
*   **The Pattern:** 80% prediction interval coverage is excessively high across all horizons: **91.5% at 7d**, **93.6% at 14d**, and **98.0% at 29d**. The actual intervals were **1.5x to 2x wider** than needed to achieve the target 80% coverage.
*   **The Market Context Explanation:** Throughout 2025, WTI experienced sharp, short-lived volatility spikes (e.g., the North American winter freeze-offs and new sanctions in mid-January that pushed WTI to $80.40, and the Israeli airstrikes on Iranian assets in mid-June). 
    Because the statistical model is backward-looking, it incorporated these high-volatility events and projected wide, conservative prediction intervals far into the future. However, the physical reality was that these risk premiums **quickly evaporated** (e.g., the US-brokered Israel-Iran ceasefire on June 24 immediately stripped the geopolitical premium, resulting in a 17% drop). The model failed to realize that in a structurally oversupplied market, price spikes are transitory, resulting in overly wide intervals that missed their 80% calibration target.

#### 2. Persistent Positive Bias (Over-Forecasting)
*   **The Pattern:** There is a persistent positive mean bias across all horizons: **+0.44 at 7d**, **+0.86 at 14d**, and **+1.49 at 29d**. 
    *   At **short-to-medium horizons (7d & 14d)**, the positive bias is highly concentrated in the **elevated volatility regime (+1.68 at 7d, +1.58 at 14d)**.
    *   At **long horizons (29d)**, the positive bias is most severe in the **medium volatility regime (+1.96)**.
*   **The Market Context Explanation:** 2025 was a structurally bearish, well-supplied market where WTI fell from $71.00 in early January to $58.52 by late November (a decline of over 17%). This steady downward drift was driven by:
    1.  **OPEC+ Cut Unwinding:** The cartel added substantial physical barrels to the market, proceeding with 411,000 bpd monthly increases in May, June, and July, followed by 137,000 bpd increases in September, October, and November.
    2.  **Slowing Demand Growth:** The IEA repeatedly downgraded global demand growth projections from 1.1 million bpd in late 2024 to just 700,000 bpd in late 2025, citing rapid electric vehicle (EV) adoption in China and macro headwinds.
    3.  **Tariff/Trade Shocks:** Repeated tariff threats (e.g., Trump's threats in late January and new global tariffs in April) created downward demand shocks.
    
    A purely statistical model like AutoARIMA assumes historical mean-reversion or flat trends. During a structural bearish shift:
    *   In **elevated regimes (short-to-medium horizons)**, the model projected that temporary price spikes would persist or mean-revert to a higher average, whereas they actually collapsed rapidly back into the downward trend.
    *   In **medium regimes (long horizons)**, the quiet, steady downward drift was misclassified by the model as a flat, range-bound trend, causing it to consistently over-forecast the price a month out by nearly $2.00.
