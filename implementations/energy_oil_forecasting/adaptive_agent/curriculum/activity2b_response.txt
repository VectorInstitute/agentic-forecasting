# 2025 WTI Backtest Analysis & Market Context Synthesis

## 1. Summary of Statistical Findings
Over the 51 historical origins in 2025, the AutoARIMA model achieved a Mean CRPS of **2.4721**, which represents a significant improvement over the naive baseline (**2.9299**). However, a closer look at the horizon-specific metrics reveals two major, persistent errors:
1. **Severe Over-Forecasting Bias:** The model exhibits a positive bias across all horizons, which escalates as the horizon lengthens:
   * **h=7d:** $+0.44$ bias
   * **h=14d:** $+0.86$ bias
   * **h=29d:** $+1.49$ bias
2. **Extreme Interval Over-Conservatism (Over-Coverage):** The model’s 80% prediction intervals are systematically too wide, leading to actual coverage rates well above the 80% target:
   * **h=7d:** $91.5\%$ coverage (average width $10.94$, but only $\pm 3.65$ or a total width of $7.30$ was needed)
   * **h=14d:** $93.6\%$ coverage (average width $15.55$, but only $8.20$ was needed)
   * **h=29d:** $98.0\%$ coverage (average width $22.31$, but only $10.72$ was needed)

## 2. Linking the News Context to the Error Patterns

By aligning the weekly market summaries with the backtest results, we can explain *why* the pure statistical model failed in these specific ways.

### A. Explaining the Over-Forecasting Bias: The 2025 Structural Bear Market
The positive bias is not a series of random errors; it is a direct consequence of a **persistent, fundamentally driven downward trend** that characterized WTI throughout 2025. 
* **The Trend:** WTI began the year in the high $60s$ to low $70s$, peaked briefly near $80.40$ in mid-January, and then embarked on a controlled, grinding slide to end the year in the high $58s$ by late November/December—a total decline of over $20\%$.
* **The Drivers:** This slide was driven by structural shifts that a backward-looking statistical model could not anticipate:
  1. **Demand-Side Destruction:** Global demand growth forecasts were repeatedly slashed. The IEA began the year projecting $1.1$ million bpd of growth but ended the year forecasting just $700,000$ bpd due to a combination of US-China tariff escalations (particularly in April, October, and November) and accelerating electric vehicle (EV) adoption in China.
  2. **Supply-Side Expansion:** Non-OPEC+ supply (particularly the US, which reached a record $13.6$ million bpd) surged. Concurrently, OPEC+ shifted its strategy from supporting prices to defending market share, implementing multiple voluntary cut unwinds of $411,000$ bpd (May, June, July) and $137,000$ bpd (October, November).
* **Statistical Failure Mode:** AutoARIMA models rely on historical averages and mean-reversion tendencies. When a market enters a structural, fundamentally driven downtrend, the model constantly expects prices to revert upward toward trailing means (e.g., reverting to $70$ when the physical market is heading to $60$). This explains why the bias grows progressively worse at longer horizons ($+1.49$ at 29d) and is highly pronounced in the "medium" volatility regime ($+1.96$ at 29d), where prices were steadily grinding downward without high-volatility spikes.

### B. Explaining the Overly Wide Intervals: Capped Volatility and Market Boundaries
The massive over-coverage ($98\%$ at 29d) indicates that the model prepared for extreme price swings that never occurred.
* **The Drivers:** 2025 was characterized by significant geopolitical threats (e.g., Israeli airstrikes on Iranian nuclear facilities in June, Red Sea transit risks, and US/UK sanctions on Russian crude). However, the price impact of these events was consistently **short-lived and capped**:
  1. **Supply Caps:** High OPEC+ spare capacity (estimated at over $6$ million bpd in the Gulf) acted as a firm ceiling on price spikes. When geopolitical tensions escalated, traders knew OPEC+ could easily fill any gap. 
  2. **Ceasefire Dynamics:** Geopolitical risk premiums evaporated rapidly (e.g., the June 13 Israeli strikes drove a spike that was completely stripped out by a US-brokered ceasefire on June 24, dropping prices by $17\%$).
  3. **Demand Floors:** Downward movements were capped by the economic breakeven for US shale producers and OPEC+'s willingness to intervene (such as their November 30 decision to pause planned increases for Q1 2026).
* **Statistical Failure Mode:** Statistical models calculate prediction intervals based on historical volatility (which included the highly volatile 2022–2024 period). Because 2025 traded in a remarkably range-bound "smile" or "tug-of-war" pattern (mostly bounded between $58$ and $72$), the actual volatility was far lower than historical averages. The model projected a standard, ever-widening uncertainty cone (reaching a massive $22.31$ average width at 29d), whereas the physical market was bounded by strong fundamental floors and ceilings.
